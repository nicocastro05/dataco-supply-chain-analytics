# Findings & Recommendations

## Executive Summary
Late deliveries are not a regional or demand-driven problem — they are a structural, 3-year-old failure affecting 57% of all items shipped. The root cause is concentrated in the shipping modes themselves, specifically First and Second Class, which are failing at rates of 100% and 80% respectively. This translates to an average loss of $0.91 in profit per late item.

## Key Findings
### 1. The Problem is Systemic, Not Regional
Late-delivery rates across all five global markets are nearly identical (ranging from 56.8% in Africa to 57.7% in Europe). Geography does not drive lateness.

### 2. First and Second Class are Broken
- **First Class:** 100% late rate (27,814 items).
- **Second Class:** 80% late rate.
- **Standard Class:** 40% late rate (the lowest, despite the highest volume).

### 3. Demand Does Not Cause Lateness
A comparison of clickstream views (demand) against late-delivery rates shows a flat trend line. Highly viewed products do not ship late any more often than low-viewed products. Lateness is a logistics issue, not a warehouse congestion issue.

### 4. The Financial Impact
- On-Time Avg Profit: **$22.50**
- Late Avg Profit: **$21.59**
- **Delta:** $0.91 lost per late item.
- Total late items: 103,400 → **$94,094 in lost margin**.

### 5. Top 5 Profit-Tied-Up Lanes
1. Europe × Standard Class ($267,375)
2. LATAM × Standard Class ($249,128)
3. Pacific Asia × Standard Class ($208,060)
4. Europe × First Class ($193,185)
5. LATAM × First Class ($183,989)

## Recommendations
1. **Immediate SLA Audit for First Class:** A 100% late rate means the SLA promise (usually 1-day shipping) is mathematically impossible for the operations network. Either relax the SLA to 2 days or stop selling First Class until fulfillment can meet it.
2. **Second Class Root Cause Analysis:** 80% is also a systemic failure. Investigate if Second Class is being fulfilled from a specific warehouse or via a carrier that is underperforming.
3. **Target the High-Volume Lanes:** While First Class is 100% late, Standard Class to Europe and LATAM ties up over $500K in profit. A 5% improvement in Standard Class lateness in these regions recovers significant margin.
4. **Category Audit:** Investigate Golf Bags & Carts (68.85% late) and Cameras (61.99% late) for category-specific picking or packaging delays.
